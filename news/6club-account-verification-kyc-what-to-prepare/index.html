6club Verification and KYC — Quick Answer

Updated September 2026.

Verification on 6club is the step where you prove that the account belongs to a real adult, and that the person asking for a withdrawal is that adult. KYC, short for know your customer, is the industry name for the same check, and it exists on every serious platform that handles money.

The documents requested vary, so this guide says what is typically asked for and tells you to follow the in-app notice for the exact list. It covers why the check exists, what to prepare, how to submit images that pass first time, why submissions get rejected, and how a verified 6club account protects the money in your wallet.

Key takeaways

  • Verification proves age, identity and ownership of the withdrawal account; it is a protection for you, not a hurdle.
  • Prepare a government photo ID in your own name; other items are requested only when the notice says so.
  • Photograph the original document flat, in daylight, with all edges visible and every detail readable.
  • Most rejections come from blur, glare, cropped edges or a name that differs from the account.
  • Upload documents only inside the app or site; never send them to anyone in a chat group.

Why 6club Verifies Identity

Three rules sit behind the 6club check. Players must be adults aged 18 and over. Each person may hold one account, registered with their own details. Withdrawals are paid to an account in the same name as the player. None of those rules can be enforced from a mobile number and a password alone, so at some point the platform needs to see who is behind the login.

Every 6club account is treated the same way, so a verification request is not a sign of suspicion. It is a routine step that also blocks the most damaging form of fraud, which is someone using a stolen login to send a balance to their own bank account. If the names do not match, that withdrawal stops.

When verification is usually requested

  • Before a first 6club withdrawal, which is the most common trigger.
  • When the name on a withdrawal account does not match the name on the profile.
  • After a change to registered details such as the mobile number.
  • During routine reviews, which can happen without any action on your part.
  • When support asks for it while resolving another issue.

Because the first trigger is a withdrawal, the smart move is to complete the process before you want money out. A 6club verification done in a calm week is easier than one done while a withdrawal is waiting.

How to Submit a Clear 6club Verification

Most first-time 6club rejections are photography problems, not identity problems. The steps below produce an image that a reviewer can read at a glance.

  1. Use the upload screen inside the app or site, and nothing else.
  2. Lay the original document flat on a plain surface in daylight; switch off the flash to avoid glare.
  3. Keep all four corners inside the frame and cover nothing with a finger or a sticker.
  4. Check that every printed detail is readable at normal size before you upload.
  5. Make sure the name is spelt exactly as it appears on your profile and on your withdrawal account.
  6. Submit an original document, not a photocopy, a screenshot of a photo or an edited image.
  7. Read the status screen and wait for an outcome before submitting again.

If the app shows a status such as pending, under review or rejected, that word is the whole story. Pending means nobody has looked yet; under review means someone has; rejected means the reason shown needs fixing. Resubmitting while a review is open only restarts the queue.

Common Reasons a Submission Is Rejected

  • Blur or glare that hides part of the text or the photograph.
  • Edges cropped so that a corner or a security feature is missing.
  • A name on the document that differs from the name on the profile or the withdrawal account.
  • An expired document.
  • A document that belongs to a family member or friend rather than the account holder.
  • An image that has been edited, filtered or assembled from screenshots.
  • Details on the profile, such as date of birth, that do not match the document.

When a 6club verification review comes back rejected, read the reason, fix that one thing, and resubmit once. Sending several different documents at the same time in the hope that one passes usually slows the review down rather than speeding it up.

How Verification Protects Your Withdrawals

The rule that money goes only to an account in the verified name is the single strongest protection a 6club balance has. If a password leaks, the thief can log in but cannot cash out to themselves. If a dispute arises, the verified record shows who owns the account. And if a withdrawal is held, the reason is nearly always one of the mismatches listed above, which the withdrawal process guide explains in more detail.

Verification also links directly to the details you entered when you signed up, described in the 6 Club registration guide. Give your real name and date of birth at that stage and the later check is simple; enter something approximate and the document will not match. How the withdrawal screen itself works, and where its status appears, is covered on the deposit and withdrawal page.

Keeping Your Documents Safe

Identity documents are valuable to criminals, so treat them with more care than the 6club login. Upload only through the screen inside the app or site. Never send a document to a messaging-app account that claims to be 6club support or a verification agent, because genuine reviews do not happen in chat groups. Once the upload is accepted, remove the photos from any shared gallery or cloud folder, or move them to a locked folder. The Google Safety Center has clear guidance on protecting personal information and recognising phishing attempts.

If someone contacts you claiming that your verification failed and asking you to resend documents by message, ignore it and check the status inside the app. If the status there is fine, the message was a scam. If you are unsure, ask through live chat or the contact page rather than replying to the message.

Frequently Asked Questions

Plain answers about identity checks and documents.

Is verification compulsory?

It is typically required before a withdrawal is paid, and it can be requested at other times during routine review. Follow the in-app notice, which states exactly what is needed.

Which documents will I be asked for?

Typically a government photo ID in your own name. Proof of address, proof that a payment account is yours, or a selfie may be requested in some cases. The in-app notice is the exact list, so do not send anything it has not asked for.

Why was my document rejected?

The most common reasons are blur, glare, cropped edges, an expired document, an edited image, or a name that differs from the profile or the withdrawal account. Read the reason shown, fix that one issue, and resubmit once.

Can I verify with a family member's documents?

No. The document must belong to the account holder, and the withdrawal account must be in the same name. Using another person's documents breaks the one-account rule and will block withdrawals rather than unlock them.

Where should I send my documents?

Only through the upload screen inside the app or site. Genuine reviews never happen over messaging apps, so any request to send documents by chat is a scam.

Final Word

Verification is a one-off job that pays for itself the first time you withdraw. Have a valid ID in your own name, photograph it properly, keep the name consistent across the profile and the payment account, and upload it only inside the app. Do that in a quiet week and your 6club withdrawals will have one fewer reason to pause.
